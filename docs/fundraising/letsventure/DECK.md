## 2 — The gap on an Indian desk

Analysts remember guidance as stories. A miss in one year is gone by the next investment-committee note. Indian tools are good at ratios and at tone. They do not keep a cited record of whether the guided number was met.

**Say:** One example you have personally lived. If you do not have one, use Infosys as the product example, not as your biography.

## 3 — What we publish

Guidance Credibility Index, 0–100.

- The quantified promise, with date and source.
- The later actual, with date and source.
- A label: met, exceeded, missed, dropped, or pending.
- A confidence tier next to the number.

No point is published without both filings. https://citealpha.com/companies/infy

**Say:** Click the Infosys revenue line for one year. Stop talking. Let them see two links.

## 7 — Why this is hard to copy

The screen is easy. Five years of Indian outcomes, each tied to the guidance filing and the results filing, is not. US guidance products do not run NSE and BSE formats or mixed-language calls. Indian data products stop before the score.

**Say:** Name Marvin Labs as the US category proof. Name Tijori as the India company that raised on filings and does a different job. Then stop.

## 8 — How an angel makes money

Desks pay an annual seat. A platform pays for the feed. The round after this one, if the pilots convert, is a seed against paid seats or a signed API. This syndicate is the first cheque, not the company-defining round.

We are not selling to retail users. Distribution to individuals stays off while counsel reviews the Research Analyst rules.

**Say:** Return comes from a small number of institutional contracts. Say that plainly. Do not show a hockey stick.
